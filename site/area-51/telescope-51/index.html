TELESCOPE 51 / LIVE RULE CARD · v2.0

Rules first. Then the record.

The V → R → C → S transition is unchanged from the published beta. The table now uses the shared jbit bank. A fixed branch/round can fund one ticket; subsequent passes are study. Opening its adjacent twin records that exposure and makes that twin a study position.

The telescope

The object stays fixed. Value collapses face cards to 10. Rank separates them. Color introduces the signed −1/+1 coordinate. Suit finishes the identity.

For candidate counts m, the increments Δ=(1/m_after)−(1/m_before) always sum from 1/3 to 1. That is the telescoping ledger; it is not automatically a probability model.

The invariant marker

The 51 active cards contain 25 black and 26 red identities. With black −1 and red +1, their signed total is +1. Outside marker Ⅰ=2♣ contributes −1 and restores zero.

The marker balances the physical pack but never enters a round.

Archive-addressing corollary

One equal-weight fiber can address the verified archive.

The v7.2 contribution profile contains a class of 18 distinct cards with exactly equal component weight. Hold its 18 occupied positions—and every card outside that class—fixed. Permuting only those 18 identities gives 18! = 6,402,373,705,728,000 arrangements with the same complete position-by-position weight profile.

14! = 87,178,291,200 < 103,800,788,359 < 15! = 1,307,674,368,000 ≤ 18!

Therefore every one-based index in the verified LMFDB archive can be injected into this single observational fiber. The cards carry an address; recovering an ordinate still requires the declared factorial ordering, the index convention, and the external archive. This does not compute a zero, extend the verified height, or imply RH.

Data boundary: LMFDB reports 103,800,788,359 zeros through height 30,610,046,000. The twelve-decimal terminal ordinate, treated as an integer under the current codec, is larger than 18! and does not remain inside this equal-weight fiber. Open the machine-readable statement →

Source and claim boundary

The exact card order is the v7.2 factorial encoding of the supplied first stored ordinate at twelve decimal places. Telescope 51 is a knowingly designed post-publication rule system applied to that order. It does not derive the ordinate, an uncomputed decimal tail, a zero theorem, or RH.

One bank. Clear prices.

jbits are the lab’s play currency. Start with 8. The reserve recovers 1 every 5:10 while below 8; winnings can carry it higher. Reloading and switching games preserve the bank.

Action Price or award
Blackjack stake 1, 2, 4 or 8 jbits
One full hidden-card reveal 6 jbits
Blackjack odds view / forecast entry 1 jbit each
Correct four-choice Memory recall without a reveal +2 jbits; up to 8 per UTC day

These are declared prices. The reveal price of 6 follows the six-bit fixed-width budget for 51 identities; the recall award of 2 follows the two-bit budget for four choices. Neither converts play into a measurement of personal memory, surprise or learning. jbits have no monetary value.
