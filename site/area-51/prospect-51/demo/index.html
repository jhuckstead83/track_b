Deal five distinct identities. Press HOLD below any to keep them. Draw once to replace the others from the remaining 46, or bank the original hand. A redraw settles immediately. All five original identities stay out of the replacement inventory, including discards.

Poker reads an unordered hand. The archive lock reads an ordered string. A swap can preserve the poker award and change the jackpot result. That distinction is the experiment.

PROBABILITY HAS A DOMAIN

A rule, not a promise.

There are 2,349,060 possible five-card subsets of this 51-identity alphabet. Four are royal flushes. Those are opening-deal counts, before the player chooses a hold. The conditional-odds panel enumerates the appropriate remaining inventory instead.

The scrolling tiles are decorative travel, not a preview of future draws or a claim about which card nearly won. Only the five center stops form your hand. The numbered-ring styling is rotor-inspired, not an Enigma simulation and not encryption. Rotors are selection maps; no stored zeta ordinate is used as a random seed. The triangular offsets are a declared design choice, not a discovered probability law.

Demo points are local to this browser tab and have no monetary value. They are not shared jbits, are not Finance holdings, and are not a site-wide jackpot. No autoplay, payment, or cash-out is provided.

ONE DIAL / TEST THE GEAR

Not every step is a permutation.

For e = a·d mod 51, coprime steps reach all identities. A shared factor removes possibilities. The dots show reachable positions, not a play prediction.

mj = 52 − j  |  ej = (ajdj + bj) mod mj

Five live depletion rotors produce 51·50·49·48·47 equally likely ordered hands under independent uniform input digits. Five independent mod-51 reels instead allow duplicate identities; that would be a different game with a different score table. Changing to three- or seven-card play likewise requires new scoring and fresh probability counts.
