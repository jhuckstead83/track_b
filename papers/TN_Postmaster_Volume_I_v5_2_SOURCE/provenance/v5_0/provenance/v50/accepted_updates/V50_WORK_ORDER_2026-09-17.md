## 2. What the certificate proves

For k = 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, with x = s(s−1), R = 2s−1 and 𝒲_k(s) = s^(2k−1) W_k(s(s−1))/(2k−1)!:

| part | statement | method |
|---|---|---|
| continuum | 𝒲_k(s) > θ_k for every real s in [1,128] | complete rational partition; every cell accepted from an outward-rounded Taylor bound with an enclosed remainder valid on the whole interval; failing cells bisected |
| tail | W_k(s(s−1)) > (α_k/2)·s^(2k)/(2s−1)^(4k−1) for every s ≥ 128 | exact rational arithmetic on the source polynomials (74A.6)–(74A.7), the Gamma margin and the complete prime bound |

with θ = (10⁻⁵, 10⁻⁸, 10⁻¹⁰, 10⁻¹², 10⁻¹⁵) and α = (7, 396, 54 000, 13 406 400, 5 258 131 200) for k = 2, …, 6. Since s ↦ s(s−1) maps [1,∞) onto [0,∞), the two parts give W_k(x) > 0 for every x > 0. No zero ordinate, verified zero height or RH assumption enters.
